# PixiJS 2D benches

Real-life 2D scenes for [benchy](https://github.com/pixijs/benchy), one file each. Each frame bench builds its scene on the stage the pixi adapter hands it (a `Renderer` on exactly the backend asked for, at the canvas's device pixels and resolution 1, and a root `stage` container), does its arithmetic in `simulate(seconds)` from the stage's seeded `rng`, and only calls into pixi in `update(dt)`. Textures are drawn in-page on a canvas (`shared.ts`). The first variant of each bench is the baseline the others are judged against. `benchy list bench` shows what the files declare, and `benchy bench/<file> --quick` measures one.

**`sprites.bench.ts`, `sprite field`** (`s10k`, `s50k`; primary `cpu.frame`). What does an app pay per frame for a field of sprites that all change every property every frame? Sprites from one four-shape sheet (one texture source, so one batch) change position, rotation, scale, alpha and tint each frame, which is the 2dbench "particles" shape. `cpu.frame` covers the setter calls in the update and the render's transform walk, batching and vertex upload of quads that all moved.

**`particles.bench.ts`, `particle container`** (`p100k`, `p500k`; primary `cpu.frame`). How cheap is the `ParticleContainer` path at hundreds of thousands of particles? `Particle`s with only position and rotation dynamic are written every frame. Scale, tint, alpha and the texture frame are uploaded once. `cpu.frame` covers the field writes and the container packing and uploading its dynamic attributes.

**`ui-tree.bench.ts`, `ui tree`** (`n5k`, `n20k`; primary `cpu.render`). What does the renderer pay to draw a large, deep, mostly static tree? About N nodes, ten levels deep under six panels, each panel clipped by a rectangular `Graphics` mask, with leaves a mix of `Sprite`, `Text` and `Graphics`. Each frame 2% of the leaves move a few pixels and one panel is dragged. `cpu.render` is the transform update, the instruction rebuild or reuse and the masks for a tree that barely changed. The update is only a few hundred setter calls.

**`graphics.bench.ts`, `graphics redraw`** (`g400`, `g2000`; primary `cpu.frame`). What does redrawing vector shapes every frame cost? N `Graphics` are cleared and redrawn each frame (a rectangle, a circle and a stroked polygon with changing sizes and colours) over 3000 `Graphics` drawn once. `cpu.frame` covers recording the commands in the update and, in the render, rebuilding the dynamic geometry (triangulating fills and strokes) and batching it with the static geometry, which is reused.

**`text.bench.ts`, `text updates`** (`t300`, `t1000`; primary `cpu.frame`). What do changing labels cost? N canvas `Text` labels, N / 10 of which get a new string each frame, sit over 10 N `BitmapText` counters whose digits all change every frame, from a bitmap font installed in-page. `cpu.frame` covers the canvas rasterisations and texture uploads plus the bitmap texts re-laid-out into glyph quads: 30 and 3000 a frame in `t300`, 100 and 10 000 in `t1000`. (Until 2026-10-21 both variants changed 30 and 3000 a frame, so the ladder only added static labels; smoke numbers before then are not comparable.)

**`batch-mix.bench.ts`, `batch breakers`** (`m6k`, `m20k`; primary `cpu.render`). What does the batcher cost when everything breaks batches at once? Sprites over 32 textures on 32 separate sources (more than a batch's texture units), are spread over 30 groups: 20 masked by `Graphics`, 4 under a `BlurFilter`, 6 plain. Inside each group the blend mode changes every 24 siblings (the sprite's index in its group, divided by 24, picks one of 8 modes), so blend breaks come in runs, not on every sprite. Before 2026-10-21 the mode came from the sprite's global index, which gave every sprite a different mode from its neighbour, so the scene was one draw per sprite; numbers from before then can't be compared. Everything drifts slowly. `cpu.render` is the batcher deciding where to break plus the encoder issuing the draws, mask stencils and filter passes. `gpu.frame` is worth reading too.

**`spawn.bench.ts`, `sprite churn`** (`c500`, `c2000`; primary `cpu.frame`). What does object churn cost? The population stays at 10 000 sprites while the oldest K are removed and destroyed and K new sprites are created and added every frame, all in the update. `cpu.frame` covers construction, `addChild`, `removeChildren`, `destroy()` and the render's handling of a child list that changes every frame.

**`startup.bench.ts`** (cpu benches, timed in Node; primary `cpu.op`). What does construction cost without a renderer? `construct 10k sprites` times `new Sprite(Texture.EMPTY)` ten thousand times. `construct 10k containers with children` builds a 100 × 99 `addChild` tree. `graphics build 1k` records 1000 `GraphicsContext`s and triangulates each with `buildContextBatches`, the geometry build `GraphicsContextSystem` runs, which needs no renderer. Text measurement is left out because it needs a 2D canvas, which Node does not have.
